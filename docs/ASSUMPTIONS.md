# Assumptions

The plans don't say everything a 3D model needs (heights, sign faces, sidewalk widths, tree positions...). Wherever they were silent, a reasonable default was picked and written down here. Numbers match the order they came up during the build; the build log (`BUILD_LOG.md`) says which phase each belongs to.

If you're a traffic engineer reading this: these are the bits most worth arguing with.

1. The origin is the center of the mainlane corridor on the Spring Cypress midline. The plans have no coordinate datum, so this stands in for "center between the feeders".
2. The plan sheets are treated as exactly 1" = 40' with no plot distortion. The scale bar confirms this to 0.1 pt.
3. The aerial basemap is NAIP 2022 at 0.6 m, used because no free recent high-resolution Harris County flight is available.
4. The plans are assumed to be State Plane South Central *grid* distances at scale 1.0. Any surface adjustment factor (about 1.0001) is ignored; it is about 0.03 m across the site.
5. Plan-to-aerial alignment is accurate to about 1 m (NAIP resolution), fitted to ground-level grass edges.
6. The site is modeled flat: all pavement at z = 0, with no grades, crowns or cross-slopes (the plans give no profiles).
7. Curbs are 6" high with a 1-ft top band; islands and roadside sit at curb height.
8. Where a curb line stops (driveways, plan edge), it is closed with a straight line. Driveway mouths are shown as raised roadside.
9. The SB frontage road south of Spring Cypress is taken to run up to the west deck edge, because the plans show no east curb there.
10. Sidewalks are not on the marking sheets. A 5-ft sidewalk directly behind the curb, within 120 ft of each intersection, is assumed.
11. Marking colors are not distinguishable in the plans, so all existing markings are white. Lane lines are drawn 6" wide.
12. Bridge: the soffit at 18 ft, deck top at 24 ft, rail height, bent locations and column size are illustrative. The plans show only the deck edges in plan view.
13. Existing poles and arms have no schedule; sheet 67 covers only the proposed LMA(5) parts. Existing mast-arm poles are taken as 30-ft galvanized poles (14" base tapering to 10") with an 8-ft luminaire arm and cobra-head at the top, matching the proposed standard. Arm lengths are measured from the plan wedges (C/H 55' + 32', E/K 44').
14. Mast arms attach at 19.5 ft above pavement and rise 1 ft to the tip. The second arm of a dual sits 0.6 ft higher.
15. Existing horizontal heads use the sheet 23 rule: bottom of housing 17'-6" above pavement, hung under the arm at the plan head position. They are 12" 3-section heads with plain black backplates; the 2" yellow border is a new-work requirement and is not shown on existing heads.
16. The 4-section vertical heads E and M are drawn at the pole/arm junction. They are modeled side-mounted on the pole with the housing centered at 16.6 ft, lenses R-Y-Y-G.
17. The ONE WAY (R6-1, 36×12) signs under heads B, G, J and P hang directly below the head. The arrow is left for G/P and right for B/J, per the head table.
18. Existing street-name signs are 6' × 1.5' green with a white border and **no text**, because the plans don't give the legend. They hang under the arm at the plan sign symbol, facing the same way as that arm's heads.
19. Luminaire arm direction comes from the plan sunburst symbol: along the arm for E, K and H (H's along the south arm); for C, along the 55-ft east arm.
20. Pedestal ped poles are 10 ft tall, 4.5" diameter, on a 1.2-ft square base. Ped heads (on pedestal and mast-arm poles) are 18" countdown housings with the bottom at 7.5 ft. One push button per ped head at 3'-9" (within the 3'-6"–4'-0" range from sheet 23), with a 9×15 ped sign above it, facing the same direction as its head.
21. The existing controller is a 4×2×5.5 ft cabinet on the plan's 6×6 ft pad. The CTMS hub is a 4×3×6 ft cabinet on its 6×6 ft pad.
22. Both "M" electrical service poles drawn on sheet 25 are modeled (16-ft steel pole with meter/disconnect and riser) and tagged remove, per the callout "verify and remove if not in service".
23. Retained R3-8 / R3-8L / R3-8R signs are ground-mounted at the plan leader arrowheads: 7 ft to the bottom of a 30×30 plate (R3-8 UMS: 36×30) on a single post, with a blank white face (no legend). Facings are inferred from the approach each one serves: UMS SB faces SB traffic; UMS NB faces NB; the center-median R3-8Ls face WB (west nose) and EB (east nose); the west-island R3-8L and the R3-8R face EB.
24. ITS ground boxes are 3.2 × 4.5 ft (plan symbol size), set flush with the raised ground. Fiber and ordinary ground boxes are not modeled.
25. Proposed mast-arm poles use the same pole, arm and luminaire geometry as Phase 4 (30-ft pole 14"→10", arm at 19.5 ft rising 1 ft to the tip, 8-ft luminaire arm), with sheet 67's Type IV fixed-mount arms and a 48-in. drilled-shaft top as the foundation. Pole centres and arm tips come from the plan outlines; the measured arms are 54.9' (A, B, C, K, M) and 59.9' (L), matching the 55'/60' labels.
26. Proposed luminaire arms point at the plan sunburst symbol (about 8 ft from each pole). Sheet 23 note 25 only says to aim them for the proper illumination pattern.
27. Proposed mast-arm heads hang at the plan housing position projected onto the arm, with the bottom of the housing 17'-6" above pavement (sheet 23 note 1), facing the plan arrow direction. Lenses are 12". Backplates extend about 5" past the housing on each side (3-section 3.9 × 1.95 ft, 4-section 4.95 × 1.95 ft, 5-section 1.95 × 6.15 ft), with the 2" yellow retroreflective border on the front face only.
28. The 4-section left-turn heads H and R are modeled as the standard TxDOT flashing-yellow-arrow head (red, yellow, flashing yellow, green left arrows). Sheet 28 labels them <R <R <Y <G; the second <R is taken as a drafting slip (see "Plan questions settled").
29. The pole-mounted 5-section heads N (pole C) and D (pole M) sit on two side brackets about 1.9 ft from the pole, toward the plan head symbol, with the housing centred at 15.8 ft (bottom about 13.1 ft). That keeps the backplate below the arm. The 17'-6" rule applies to mast-arm heads; the plans give no height for pole-mounted heads. Lenses top to bottom: R, Y, G, Y right arrow, G right arrow (sheet 28).
30. Street-name signs S1 (8 × 1.5 ft) and S2 (4 × 1.5 ft) hang under the arm at the plan sign symbol, facing the same way as that arm's heads, top 0.6 ft below the arm. Legends are from sheet 28 ("Spring Cypress Rd", "SH 249"), white on green, set in Blender's default sans font because ClearviewHwy is not available. Cap height is about 0.72 ft (descenders included), slightly under the plan's 8".
31. R6-1 ONE WAY signs S3 (left) and S4 (right), 36 × 12, hang under the arm at the plan symbol between heads (S3 on arms A and K, S4 on arms C and M). Each faces that arm's approach. The arrow points along the one-way feeder as that approach sees it. Legend "ONE WAY" in black on the white arrow.
32. Radar units are generic boxes: presence about 11 × 9 × 3 in., advance about 15 × 11 × 4 in. Pole-mounted presence units (PR1, PR2, PR5, PR6) are on a short bracket at 22 ft, aimed from the pole toward the plan symbol (this matches their scheduled approach). Arm-mounted units (PR3, PR4, ADV1–ADV4) hang about 1.1 ft below the arm at the plan symbol. ADV units aim upstream along their scheduled approach. PR3 (WB) and PR4 (EB) aim at their scheduled stop-bar zones, because their plan symbols are drawn along the arm and don't show an aim. Sheet 23 notes 39, 43 and 44 leave final placement and aim to the manufacturer.
33. ADV3 is drawn on arm M on sheet 27, although callout M lists only a presence radar. It is modeled where the plan places it.
34. APS ped heads use the Phase 4 ped-head geometry (bottom 7.5 ft). Each head faces across its crosswalk toward the partner pole (pairs D–E, F–G–H, I–J, S–T, R–Q–P, N–O; G and Q have two heads). Push buttons use the Phase 4 geometry at 3'-9". Their facing follows the sheet 28 sign type: R10-3eL faces the crossing direction +90°, R10-3eR −90°; R10-3eD (G, Q) is set perpendicular to the first crosswalk. So proposed push buttons don't simply face the same way as their head, unlike assumption 20 for the existing ones.
35. Controller U: the plan's 6 × 6 ft pad, an 18-in. base (sheet 23 note 8) and a 4 × 2 × 5.2 ft cabinet at the plan's inner rectangle. The battery back-up cabinet (`P_CTRL_U_BBU`, 1.5 × 1.6 × 3.4 ft) is side-mounted on the east face. The plans list the BBU but don't draw it.
36. Electrical service V (Type D) reuses the Phase 4 service-pole geometry (16-ft pole, meter, disconnect, riser, weatherhead) at the plan hexagon. No service drop is modeled.
37. S5 R3-8L (30 × 30) is ground-mounted at the plan symbol on the median east of the NB intersection, using the same 7-ft clearance post as assumption 23. It faces west, serving EB traffic, as the plan plate symbol shows. The legend is simplified: a left-turn arrow plus "ONLY", and a through-left arrow.
38. Proposed markings are the complete marking plan on sheets 40–41 and replace the existing markings. Line widths: 6 in. for all lines except the gore hatching (12 in., H). The 8-in. (E) lines are not distinguished from the 6-in. ones. Dotted guide lines use the same 2.67-ft on/off pattern as Phase 3. The black contrast (shadow) markings (C) are shown as plain white.
39. Marking colour: yellow where a line runs within 3 ft of a median island (same rule as Phase 3), otherwise white. Raised pavement markers (J) are 4-in. square buttons at the plan symbol positions, amber next to medians and white elsewhere.
40. Type 1 and Type 5 curb ramps: a 5.6-ft-wide ramp at the plan pad, with a 2-ft detectable warning at the gutter and a 6-ft run up to the 6-in. curb level (1:12). Flares are simplified to vertical side walls. Type 5's mountable curb is not shown separately.
41. Type 21 (median) and Type 22 (channel island) ramps are modeled as street-level cut-throughs with detectable warnings at each end. Type 22 is a walkway from the far pad to the corner between the two crosswalk pads, as drawn. The standard sheets weren't available to confirm the exact Type 21/22 geometry.
42. Detectable warnings are yellow (TxDOT allows any colour with strong contrast; the plans don't say).
43. The ramp at pad 2 (NW corner across the SB right-turn channel) falls on an area the Phase 3 surface treats as pavement (the curb there wasn't closed). It is modeled as a freestanding ramp with a 4-ft landing rather than cut into a curb.
44. The ramp cuts in the existing curb and raised surfaces are a non-destructive modifier and appear in the existing view too. This is acceptable because the existing ped crossings also had ramps at these corners.
45. S6 R1-5BL (36 × 36) is ground-mounted at the plan symbol with the same 7-ft clearance post as assumption 23, facing traffic approaching the channel stop bar. The face is simplified: a red octagon with "STOP", "HERE" / "FOR" text, a down-left arrow and a simple pedestrian figure.
46. Underground and flush items from sheets 26–27 (conduit, bores, ground boxes, fiber re-route, callouts 1–41) are not modeled, by decision.
47. Context buildings are OSM footprints (as of 2026-11-06) within the model extent + 200 ft, extruded flat-roofed from the aerial level. OSM gives no heights or levels here, so heights are assumed by type: restaurant / fast food 18 ft, bank 20 ft, convenience store 16 ft, big-box retail (department store, supermarket or > 25,000 ft²) 26 ft, other 1-storey commercial 18 ft, outbuildings < 600 ft² 10 ft. Had OSM given `building:levels`, the rule would be 14 ft per level + 4 ft parapet.
48. The Mobil fuel canopy (OSM `building=roof`) is a 2-ft slab at 15–17 ft on three 1.5-ft square columns along its long axis; pump islands are not modeled.
49. Buildings visible in the aerial but missing from OSM are not modeled.
50. Trees are derived from NAIP 2022 (0.6 m): canopy = ExG > 14 and darker than mown grass, with thin shadow slivers removed. Positions and crown sizes come from packing circles into the canopy mask, so individual trunks are approximate (dense woods are represented by evenly packed crowns). Species can't be told from NAIP; all trees are generic broadleaf.
51. Tree height = 2.1 × crown radius + 8 ft (± 2 ft random), limited to 16–45 ft; crown radius 5–16 ft. Trunks are kept ≥ 3 ft from pavement, curb and sidewalk, ≥ 4 ft from buildings, ≥ 12 ft from ramp pads and out of the bridge footprint + 8 ft.
52. Trees, buildings and the canopy stand on the aerial level (z = −0.10 m), also where they fall inside the raised roadside; the trunk passes through the 6-in. surface.
53. Surface textures are generic tileable textures (no site photos), with wear spread uniformly; there are no location-specific wheel-path or stop-bar stains. Asphalt is modeled as weathered asphalt, although the aerial suggests the frontage roads may be concrete.
54. Pavement-marking wear is about 8% of the paint area, uniform across existing and proposed markings (new markings would in reality be crisp).
55. Lighting is a clear mid-morning on 15 April 2026, 10:00 CDT (sun elevation 38.9°, azimuth 102° true). The sky is a stylised clear-sky gradient, not a physical sky model.
56. Driver's eye 3.5 ft above pavement, 8 ft behind the upstream edge of the proposed stop bar (front bumper at the bar), centred in the lane. One camera per approach, in lane 2 from the left; the visibility check covers every lane.
57. Lane centres come from the sheet 40/41 lane lines (solid and dotted) in the first 60 ft upstream of each stop bar; a space with 12-in. hatching (the NB-feeder gore) is not a lane. Driving direction follows the local lane lines.
58. Head visibility: a head counts as visible when at least 5 of 9 rays to its face are clear. The posted speeds from the build doc stand in for the 85th-percentile speed in MUTCD Table 4D-2 (45 mph → 460 ft, 50 mph → 540 ft). Vehicles, grades and anything outside the model are not considered.
59. Pedestrian eye 5 ft above the walking surface, 5 ft behind the first crosswalk bar (on the new ramp or landing), facing the far ramp. The NB east crosswalk has no new ramp pad at its north end; that camera stands at the curb end of the crosswalk near ped pole R. Where the ground raycast falls in a gap beside a ramp mesh, the surface is taken as pavement level.
60. The APS close-up uses pole D (PB1, R10-3eL); its sign face is blank as modelled in Phase 5.
61. Presence-radar (PR) zones cover the full approach width at the stop bar, from the stop bar to 50 ft upstream, clipped to pavement. The coverage check assumes a generic presence radar with a ±45° field of view and 140-ft range.
62. Advance-radar (ADV) zones are the dilemma zone 2.5–5.5 s of travel upstream of the stop bar at the posted speed, inside a 12° beam on the radar's modelled aim and within the approach width. Outside the model extent the zone is the stop-bar width carried along the road axis, not clipped to pavement.
63. Radar zones are illustrative footprints 3 cm above the pavement (13 cm above the aerial outside the model extent); the actual zones are configured by the vendor.
64. Change-view colours: removed red, new green, as a 60 % tint over the original material; remaining equipment, the existing roadway surfaces and context are untinted. Existing and new markings overlap, with the new ones drawn on top.
65. Radar-zone labels use Blender's default font, converted to mesh.
66. The GLB is exported without Draco (6.06 MB, well under the 20 MB target), so the viewer needs no decoder. All textures are embedded as JPEG at quality 85, including the aerial (2000 × 1340, 0.41 MB).
67. Trees are exported with GPU instancing (EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing): the 131 trees become 8 instanced nodes, so per-tree tags (crown size, height) are not in the GLB; the 8 nodes carry a shared tree note.
68. The viewer's lighting approximates the Eevee renders (hemisphere light + sun from the same direction, sun shadows near the interchange, no global illumination), so colours and brightness differ a little from the Blender renders.
69. The viewer shows the Blender ortho cameras (Top, Radar top) as a high, narrow (30°) perspective view with the same ground width, not a true orthographic view.
70. The viewer's Change mode tints in three.js (material colour 60 % toward the Phase 7 red/green, plus a small emissive), approximating the Blender change view. Roadway surfaces are a separate layer toggle, so they stay visible in every mode.
71. The KMZ uses plain diffuse colours (no textures); lens emission is folded into the colour. Markings and ramps are lifted 0.10 m. Each model is rigid and flat, while Google Earth's terrain isn't, so parts may float or sink slightly. Equipment to remain is in the Existing folder (it also stays in the proposed condition).
72. KMZ placement uses the Phase 2 origin and heading 1.670°; it agrees with the Phase 2 plan→UTM transform within 0.07 m, so the absolute accuracy is still the ~1 m of the NAIP alignment (assumption 5).
73. The final driver's-eye render is the SB-feeder approach to the SB intersection (CAM_P7_Drv_SBint_SB, 50 mph, the fastest approach).
